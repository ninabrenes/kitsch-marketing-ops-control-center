# Case study: from marketing heroics to operating rhythm

## Context

This project was created for a Marketing Operations / Chief of Staff opportunity at Kitsch. The role asks for more than reporting: it requires an operator who can connect launch execution, measurement, budget governance, leadership decisions, and cross-functional follow-through.

## My approach

I treated the public web as an incomplete but useful research layer. I mapped observable signals across Kitsch’s website, commerce footprint, social accounts, search demand, public advertising libraries, retailers, editorial coverage, and competitors. I then separated the evidence into five confidence classes so a persuasive portfolio artifact would never masquerade as access to internal performance.

The interface was designed around a repeating decision grammar:

> Observation → interpretation → decision → owner → validation metric

## What I built

### Leadership control center

A compact executive view that surfaces the strongest public signals and turns them into business questions. It deliberately leaves CAC, ROAS, MER, LTV, contribution, conversion, and repeat purchase blank until first-party sources are connected.

### Marketing operating system

A capability map connects work management, Shopify, Klaviyo, GA4, paid platforms, attribution, BI, finance/procurement, and knowledge management. Each layer has a purpose, owner, source-of-truth rule, and handoff.

### Closed-loop workflows

Launch, budget, creative-learning, lifecycle, and leadership-decision workflows each define a trigger, flow, accountable owner, service level, automation opportunity, and human approval boundary.

### Growth and portfolio intelligence

The analysis connects category demand, public social-commerce momentum, product doorways, second-category migration, channel roles, competitive territories, advertising pressure, and durable content strategy.

## The non-obvious insight

Kitsch may not have an activity problem. It may have a **compression problem**.

An expanding assortment, seasonal drops, collaborations, promotions, retailers, marketplaces, creative formats, and measurement tools can all be individually rational while collectively creating customer confusion and organizational heroics. The operating opportunity is to simplify, sequence, and create explicit handoffs.

That thesis produced four proposed diagnostics:

- **Launch Collision Index:** how many messages, launches, dependencies, and creative requests compete in the same week?
- **Fragrance Wedge Quality:** does the first fragrance purchase create profitable full-size and cross-franchise adoption?
- **Marketplace-to-Relationship Gap:** how often does marketplace discovery become a consented, identifiable owned-channel relationship?
- **Measurement Truth Tax:** how long does the organization spend reconciling numbers before it can make a decision?

## What I would connect next

With authorization, I would connect Shopify orders and refunds, Klaviyo profiles and lifecycle events, GA4 behavior, paid-platform spend and creative IDs, retailer/marketplace exports, inventory, COGS, fulfillment, channel fees, finance actuals, and the team’s project-management system.

The first pilot would be one live launch, one weekly exception view, one decision log, and one 30-day results review. Automation would follow only after definitions and ownership are trusted.
